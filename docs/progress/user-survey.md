# User survey — DME Explorer

For the *real user feedback* extra credit (+1). Written to produce **numbers you can
put on a slide**, not just opinions: three timed tasks with right answers, then
short ratings.

Bilingual: Thai first (most first-year students answer more honestly in Thai),
English underneath. Paste both lines into each Google Forms question.

- **Who:** 5–8 first-year DME students (or Mathayom 6 students considering DME).
- **How long:** 8 minutes with the site open, ~4 minutes if they only answer ratings.
- **Where:** in person is best — you see where they hesitate. A shared link works too.
- **Device:** ask at least 2 people to use a phone, so the responsive claim on Slide 5 is tested.

## Turning responses into slide content

Compute after collecting:

| Number | How |
|---|---|
| **Task success rate** | correct answers ÷ attempts, per task |
| **Median difficulty** | median of Q4 and Q6 (target: ≤ 2) |
| **Chatbot usefulness** | mean of Q7 |
| **Would recommend** | % answering "Definitely" |
| **Phone usability** | mean of Q12 |

Put on **Slide 9** of the deck:
1. One line of numbers: *"5 first-year students, 8 of 10 task attempts correct, median difficulty 2/5."*
2. **Two verbatim quotes** — one positive, one critical. Keep the critical one; it
   is what makes the feedback look real.
3. **One fix you shipped because of it**, with the commit. A graded demo that says
   "users stumbled here, so we changed it, here is the commit" scores better than
   any rating average.

If a task success rate comes out low, that is a finding, not a failure — present it
as the next thing to fix on Slide 14.
